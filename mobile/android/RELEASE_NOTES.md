**OksaSDR for Android — beta 96.** A FlexRadio client for Android phones and tablets, built on the [AetherSDR](https://github.com/aethersdr/AetherSDR) engine: the same app as on the iPhone and iPad (TestFlight), from the same code.

### New in beta 96
- **SmartLink after a network change, steadier** (roadmap 84): beta 95 came back from every switch between Wi-Fi and the mobile network by itself, but once its new connection to the SmartLink server took 37 s (the first packets lost while the network came up), and going from 5G back to Wi-Fi a session could stay on the old network with the display frozen (thanks Rich Yost N2RY: the phone kept the old connection, the radio sent the spectrum and audio to the old address). A session on the network that went is now made again over the new one (back in about 8 s), a server connection not up in 8 s is made again, and a connect cancelled half way no longer leaves the next one stuck at *Connecting…*.
- **Keys for TUNE, the ATU and a two-tone tune** (roadmap 85; Christoph Steinert DM5HF): on a keyboard (also a Bluetooth keyboard or DeX) **T** turns TUNE on and off, **Shift+T** tunes with the radio's two-tone test signal (its own tune mode is put back afterwards) and **A** runs an ATU tune. They transmit, so the transmit lock holds them as it holds PTT.
- **The slice back where it was after a reconnect** (roadmap 87; Rich Yost N2RY): on a radio that takes one station, a network change could bring the app back on the radio's default frequency and mode (Rich: 18.130 USB became 14.111 CW), with a second slice beside it. When the app has to make the slice again after a reconnect, it now puts it back on the frequency, mode and filter it had, and makes no second one.
- **The DX cluster after a network change** (roadmap 86): when the cluster refuses the new login because it still holds this device's login from the network before, the app says so (it used to ask whether the call was logged in from another device), and the log shows what the cluster said.

### Still new from beta 95
- **SmartLink after a network change, now in seconds** (roadmap 84): beta 94 made the SmartLink connection again after a network change, but the sign-in was renewed over an HTTPS connection kept from the old network, and it waited out a 15 s timeout (on the developer's iPad twice) before the app was back. Those kept connections are now dropped with the network, so the sign-in and the radio come back in a few seconds (tested against the FLEX: signed in again 1.2 s after the change, the dropped session back in 8.6 s).

### Still new from beta 94
- **SmartLink comes back after a network change** (roadmap 84; found on the developer's iPad): moving between Wi-Fi and the mobile network or a phone's hotspot while the app ran left it at *Connecting…* with *SmartLink server not reachable. Retrying…* and no button working, until the app was closed completely. The connection to SmartLink's server stayed on the network that had gone, and the request for the radio sent on it was never answered. Now a network change makes that connection again at once, a request without an answer in 10 s does the same and asks again, a dropped remote session connects again by itself (showing *Connecting…*), and **Cancel** beside *Connecting…* on the Connect page stops a connect. If the radio closes a remote connection before it answers, the app tries once more by itself, and the log tells more of each remote connection (the ports, the TLS session, whether the radio answered) for reports like Mohammed A71AT's.

### Still new from beta 93
- **MIDI controllers on Android** (roadmap 54; asked by Hanno Vogels DK3HV and Michael Walker): Radio Setup › MIDI takes a controller such as Lynovation's CTR2-MIDI as a VFO knob and buttons, as on the iPhone and iPad: Learn, the controller's map import (SmartSDR .map or AetherSDR .xml), F1–F12, transmitting from it only when allowed. A USB controller shows when plugged in (USB-C or an OTG adapter). Android has no pairing screen for Bluetooth MIDI, so **Pair a Bluetooth MIDI controller…** makes the app search for 15 s: switch the controller on, tap it when it joins the list. The first search asks for *Nearby devices* (Android 12 and later; *Location* on older Android, which it needs for any Bluetooth search); allow it and tap again. A controller used once opens again by itself. Not yet tried with a real controller on Android: reports welcome.
- **Several SmartLink accounts and favourite radios** (idea: Ian Gilchrist VK1HF): **+ Account** on the Connect page signs in another FlexRadio account and keeps the first; a row of buttons changes between them. **☆** beside a radio makes it a favourite: the favourites of every account stay at the top, and a tap connects, changing the account first when needed. Sign out signs out one account.
- **Spelling the call by voice, tidier**: a call cut by a word the recogniser did not understand is no longer put in the field (*Not sure of the call*: spell it again), the recording may run 15 s (8 s cut a slowly spelled call), and the five alternatives the recogniser gives are now different hearings rather than the same one spelled *oscar* / *oskar*.

### Still new from beta 92
- **Spelling the call by voice works on Android, with the app's own recogniser**: betas 84 to 91 used the phone's own speech recognition, and on the developer's phone it could not take the alphabet (Google's over the network wrote *"Oscar Auto 6 Oscar kilo Seattle"* for *Oskar Hotel six Oscar Kilo Sierra Alfa*, the on-device one heard speech and answered nothing). Now the app records you as for a voice macro and its own offline recogniser, **Vosk** (alphacephei, Apache 2.0, the small English model inside the app), reads the recording listening only for the alphabet's words, the digits, *slash* / *stroke* / *portable* and the report, so *Hotel* cannot come out as *Auto* whatever the accent. Nothing leaves the phone and no network is needed; it works on every Android the app runs on, not only 13 and later, and with a Bluetooth headset when that is the app's microphone. On this developer's PC, spelled calls in two synthesized English voices came right 20 times in 20, and 19 in 20 with loud background noise. The first use copies the speech model (68 MB) out of the app, a few seconds once. The APK is about 40 MB larger for the model.

### Still new from beta 91
- **Spelling the call by voice: the phone's own recogniser without hints**: beta 90's hints made the phone's on-device recogniser drop out at every press (*Speech recognition failed (11)*) or hang. They are gone; the recogniser that checks for English now also listens (beta 89 closed it and opened another at once, which also made it drop out), English is checked once while the app runs, and if the on-device recogniser still drops out before it listens, the phone's other recogniser takes the press.

### Still new from beta 90
- **Spelling the call by voice: hints for the phone's own recogniser**: beta 88 got text from Google's recogniser over the network at last, but it writes plain English words for the alphabet (on the developer's phone *"Oscar Auto 6 Oscar kilo Seattle"* for *Oscar Hotel Six Oscar Kilo Sierra Alpha*). The phone's own recogniser, used when it has English installed (beta 89), now gets the alphabet's words as hints, as the iPhone's does; Google's over the network gets none (with them it wrote nothing in beta 87). The log has each thing the recogniser wrote on its own line.

### Still new from beta 89
- **Spelling the call by voice uses the phone's own English when it has it**: beta 85 asked to download the English model for the phone's on-device recogniser, but betas 87 and 88 did not use that recogniser. Now it is chosen first when English is installed on the phone (nothing leaves the phone), then the Google app's, then the default one; the log says whether English is installed, downloading or missing. Please try it and send the log (Log › Send to the developer) if the call still does not come.

### Still new from beta 88
- **Spelling the call by voice, the next step on Android**: with beta 87 the phone's recogniser heard the speech (the log showed it) but wrote nothing, as the developer's phone's default recogniser has no English. Beta 88 uses the Google app's recogniser where the phone has it (English over the network), keeps the text it gives on the way when its final answer comes back empty, and the log names the recognisers on the phone, the one used and what it heard.

### Still new from beta 87
- **Spelling the call by voice listens on Android**: in betas 84 to 86 the app recorded you and handed the recording to the phone's recogniser, and Google's recogniser gave nothing back for it (on the developer's phone *Nothing was heard*, whatever was said). Now the phone's own recogniser listens to the microphone itself while you hold **🎤**, as in any Android app with a microphone button; the line under the field says *Starting…* until it listens, then *Listening…*. It is usually Google's recogniser, on the phone where it has English, otherwise over the network. The log tells what the recogniser did, for reports.

### Still new from beta 86
- **Spelling the call by voice knows more words** (thanks Michael Walker): beta 84 and 85 read only the ITU / NATO alphabet, so *Victor Alpha three sugar Kilo* left the field empty. Now also the older Allied alphabet (*Able*, *Baker* … *Sugar*, *King* … *Zebra*), the ITU's of 1927 (*Amsterdam*, *Baltimore* …), the places hams spell with (*America*, *Boston*, *Canada*, *Japan*, *Kilowatt*, *London*, *Mexico*, *Radio*, *Texas* … *Zanzibar*), the letters themselves (*double you*, *zed*), *fox trot* and *ex-ray* as two words, *forward slash* and *maritime mobile* (/MM), *to* / *for* / *won* / *ate* between spelled characters as the recogniser may write the digits, *fifty nine* as a report, and *uh* / *um* left out. The manual's Log tab section lists them all.

### Still new from beta 85
- **While spelling the call by voice only this device's audio goes quiet** (beta 84 muted the slice on the radio, for every station on it), and a press the system cancels ends the listening as letting go does.
- **Type the RIT or XIT offset** (thanks Sebastian Schumann DG5CST): a tap on the value in the DSP tab's RIT or XIT row opens a field: *1.34* (kHz) or *-0.5*, a whole number of 10 or more as Hz (*1340*), or −1 / +1 / +2 / +3 / +5 kHz; *Reset* sets 0, and a value set turns it on. **Hold SPLIT › Other…** splits by any distance, "up 1.34" with a second slice or by XIT.
- **Big buttons: the mode** at the top right opens the mode chooser, as on the main screen.

### Still new from beta 84
- **Spell the call by voice** (idea: Michael Walker): **🎤** beside the call field on the Log tab and in Big buttons' log. Hold it, spell the call with the international alphabet (*Victor Alpha Three Mike Whiskey*, *niner*, *stroke portable*) and let go; a quick tap listens until a second tap. Android's own speech recognition (on the device where it offers that) turns it into text, the call goes into the field and a report said after it (*five nine*) into Sent; nothing is logged until you tap Log. It mutes the radio's audio here while listening and does not work while transmitting. Needs Android 13 or later; the first time Android may download its English speech model. National alphabets may follow.
- **Edit or delete a logged contact** (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF): a tap on one in the Log tab's list opens it: the call, date and time, frequency, mode, reports, name, comment and the parks, then *Save*, or *Delete* and *Delete it*. It changes this device's log; a copy that already went to Wavelog / Cloudlog, the QRZ.com logbook, the shared ADIF file or Logger32 is not changed there (the dialog names them).
- **× in the call field** clears it, and a **Name** field beside Comment takes the looked-up first name until you type one; it is logged as ADIF NAME (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF).

### Still new from beta 83
- **POTA self-spot from the Log tab** (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF): with **My park** set to POTA and your park's reference typed, **Self-spot** beside it shows what goes out (*OH6OKSA at US-1234, 14285.0 kHz SSB*, the transmit slice's frequency and mode, FT8 or FT4 while FTx runs), takes a comment if you like, and on **Spot** posts it to pota.app as a public spot under your call; POTA's answer shows under the row. With two parks (*US-1234, US-5678*) it asks which. At most one spot in 30 s, and nothing is sent by itself. (pota.app's spot interface is unofficial: if POTA changes it, the answer says so.)
- **Big buttons › Log** (thanks Michael Walker): **Log** beside the mode opens the call in big type and the two reports with big **−** / **+** (59, 599 or the FTx report), then **Log** (with My park, as on the Log tab; the call is the Log tab's too). **Cancel** goes back, PTT stays at the bottom, and with a keyboard Return logs and Esc cancels.
- **The KPA1500's Tune in STANDBY** (thanks Rich Yost N2RY): Rich's log showed the tune carrier going out at about 775 W for the last 0.7 s of each tune: once the ATU had tuned, the amplifier was back in OPERATE and amplified the carrier the app still held to read the SWR. Tune now puts the amplifier in STANDBY first, as the RF2K-S's Tune does, stops the carrier if it ever reads OPERATE, and after the tune says *Put it in OPERATE when you are ready*. The other amplifiers were checked for the same: the RF2K-S already tunes in STANDBY, the Tuner Genius XL keys the radio itself, and the radio's own TUNE keeps the outputs chosen under Radio Setup › Transmit › *Inhibit during TUNE* open.
- **Receive audio in order** (upstream AetherSDR's fix for #6051): audio packets that arrive out of order, as they often do over a VPN, LTE or Wi-Fi, are put back in order before they are played (a gap is waited for up to 30 ms); before, a packet that came late sounded like a lost one. Also from upstream: a late spectrum or waterfall packet no longer drops the frame being drawn, and a small packet to the radio every 5 s keeps a firewall's return path for the spectrum and audio open.
- **RADE and the voice keyer** (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF): F1–F12 played the radio's own voice keyer (SmartSDR+'s DVK) while RADE was on, and the radio sends that as analog audio, so it went out as plain SSB on a RADE frequency. They now say why and do not play it there; the app's own recordings (on a radio without SmartSDR+) go out as RADE like your voice.
- (iPhone, iPad and Mac only: **MIDI Learn names a control another function has** (thanks Rich Yost N2RY): Radio Setup › MIDI asks *Replace* or *Learn again* instead of silently taking it over. MIDI controllers come to Android later.)

### Still new from beta 82
- **Spot from the Log tab** (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF): **Spot** beside Log sends the call to the DX cluster the app is logged in to (Controls › View › DX cluster), at the slice frequency. It asks first and shows the exact line that goes out (*DX 14154.0 K1ABC SSB*), as it is a public post under your call; the remark is the mode, with their park first when you have set one (*POTA US-1234 SSB*), and you can change it. Under the row: *Spotted on the cluster* when the spot comes back, the cluster's own answer if it refuses it, or *Not seen back* after 30 s. Without the cluster Spot is dimmed and a tap says where to turn it on.
- **Big buttons: Step in two halves and a larger Exit** (thanks Michael Walker): the left half of the Step bar takes a smaller step (▼), the right half a larger one (▲), the step shown across the middle; at the smallest or largest step its half is dimmed. **Exit** is as large as the slice buttons beside it, and leaves after half the hold it needed.
- **Noise line in the AGC panel**: the noise floor line's switch was only in Controls › View, easy to miss; now it is also **Noise line** under Auto and Follow in the AGC panel (tap the RF GAIN label or the AGC in the info row). The line is thicker in 2D, where it sank into the trace's own noise.
- **The Tuner Genius XL first on the Amp tab** (thanks Hanno Vogels DK3HV): above the Power Genius XL. A Tune the radio refuses while the app's own link to the tuner is down now says so (it went through the radio; with the link up Tune goes to the tuner directly) and what to check, not only the radio's code.
- **SPLIT 1 kHz up in DIGU, DIGL and RTTY**, as in CW (5 kHz put an FT8 transmit slice outside the FT8 segment); voice stays 5 kHz, and holding SPLIT still chooses.
- (iPhone, iPad and Mac only: **F1–F12 and Stop on a MIDI controller's buttons**, Radio Setup › MIDI › Macros, thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF. MIDI controllers come to Android later.)

### Still new from beta 81
- **AGC-T follows the noise** (AGC panel › **Follow**, as Thetis's Auto AGC): from Auto's measurement of each band and mode, AGC-T moves as the noise floor rises and falls, at dusk on 80 m, at sunrise on 40 m or on another antenna. It waits, and says why, until Auto has measured the band and mode, with a filter much wider or narrower than measured with, in AGC off, while you transmit and in FM. FM has no AGC on the radio, so Auto is dimmed there.
- **Noise floor line** (Controls › View): the noise floor the app reads as a dashed yellow line across the spectrum with its dBm, in 2D and 3D. Off by default.
- The passband stays in view as the display follows your tuning (an LSB slice tuned down showed only part of its filter at a narrow span).
- The Elecraft KPA1500's Tune ends when the amplifier says its tune is over, and the log says each second what it showed (thanks Rich Yost N2RY).
- (iPhone, iPad and Mac only: pairing a Bluetooth MIDI controller no longer crashes on iOS 27.)
