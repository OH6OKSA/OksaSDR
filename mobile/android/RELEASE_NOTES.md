**OksaSDR for Android and Linux — beta 122.** A FlexRadio client for Android phones and tablets, built on the [AetherSDR](https://github.com/aethersdr/AetherSDR) engine: the same app as on the iPhone and iPad (TestFlight), from the same code. One app runs the whole station: the radio, FT8 / FT4, CW, RTTY, PSK, RADE, the log and the spots, with no WSJT-X, logging program or cluster client beside it.

**Linux** (new on this page): `OksaSDR-….AppImage` under *Assets* is the same app for a Linux desktop (x86_64; Ubuntu 22.04, Linux Mint 21 or newer). Download it, allow it to run (right-click › *Properties* › *Permissions* › tick *Allow executing file as program*, or `chmod +x` in a terminal; without it a double-click does nothing) and start it; see [the tester guide](https://github.com/OH6OKSA/OksaSDR/blob/mobile/oksasdr/docs/mobile/TESTING.md#linux).

### New in beta 122
- **A Bluetooth headset is not given up** (roadmap 214; found on the developer's phone with beta 121). When a headset's call link dropped by itself, beta 121 could move the sound to the phone and never return to the headset until Audio output was set to *Speaker* and back to *Auto*. Now the app keeps asking for the headset for as long as Android lists it: five quick tries, then one every 20 seconds. A headset that was switched off or went out of reach gives the sound to the phone and is taken again within five seconds of coming back. During a phone call the app leaves the headset to the call and takes it again afterwards. And coming back to the app no longer interrupts receive for a second and a half when the headset was already playing.
- **A Bluetooth headset goes on playing when the app leaves the front** (roadmap 218; asked by Mattias SM6YEC, the cause found on the developer's phone). With a headset used hands-free the sound stopped for two to four seconds every time the screen went dark or another app came to the front, and before beta 121 after every transmission. The cause was in the library the app is built on: when the microphone closed, it took back the app's own request for the headset together with its own. The app now asks for the headset in a way that this does not touch, so the headset should go on playing with the screen off, the cover closed or another app in front; if its link drops all the same, it is taken again at once and no longer after a second and a half. No microphone is kept open in the background.
- **The app no longer stands still when a headset is switched off** (roadmap 221; found on the developer's phone). With the headset's *Media audio* on in Android's Bluetooth settings, which is how most headsets are set, switching the headset off or losing it while connected could stop the whole app for some 15 seconds, long enough for the radio to drop the connection. The app asked Android's audio service about the headset from its main thread, and that service is slow to answer while such a headset goes. Those questions are now asked on the side: the app goes on and stays connected (seen on the developer's phone), and the sound moves to the phone once Android lets it, which can still take those 15 seconds. Beta 121 very likely had this too.

### Still new from beta 121
- **A Bluetooth headset as a hands-free set** (roadmap 201; Mattias SM6YEC, and measured on the developer's phone with a Jabra Evolve2 65). A headset used over its call link alone (*Media audio* off in Android's Bluetooth settings) had a second or more of silence after each PTT, lost receive whenever the microphone closed (a tab closed, the app to the background), and after the background could stay silent until Audio output was changed by hand. Now, with Audio output on *Auto*: while the app is in front it holds the headset's microphone open, so the headset's link stays up and receive is back at once after PTT (Android shows its microphone indicator for as long; the audio goes to your radio as with VOX, up to some 50 MB an hour over mobile data, and the radio transmits only at PTT); a link that drops is taken again by the app itself, up to five times; back in front after the background such a headset's route is renewed once (a second and a half of silence); and when Android does not open the link a notice says so and what to try. After *Bluetooth media* is chosen while connected, receive is opened once more a second and a half later, when Android has let go of the call link (it stayed silent on a tester's phone after a change from *Auto*). Measured on one phone and headset; the app's own keeping of the link is new in this beta. If your headset behaves worse than in beta 120, send the log (Log › *Send to the developer*) and name the headset.
- **PTT › Wide with the phone on its side** (roadmap 204): PTT stands in the middle of the bottom row and takes the room the row has, the other buttons at its two sides. Before, *Wide* was as *Right* there.
- **Receive audio that could not start is tried again** (roadmap 181): after a call or another app's sound, or a reconnect in the background, a receive stream that did not open was left shut until the app came to the front. It is tried again every five seconds, and at once in front.
- **The 3D view is not given up for a busy moment** (roadmap 181): it was switched to the 2D waterfall as *too slow on this GPU* when the app was merely busy, as right after an audio route change. The graphics chip is now judged by how long a frame itself takes to draw.
- **When the app stops answering, the log says where** (roadmap 203; a foldable's outer screen turned on its side stood still twice on the developer's phone): once the app has stood still for 16 seconds the log has where its threads were, also after Android has ended the app (at the next start, under *previous run*). Nothing changes on the screen.
- **The log's lines begin `oksa.…`**, the app's own name for its channels, and **the demo radio is *OksaSDR Demo*** (roadmap 192). A warning line written at every start since beta 119 is gone (roadmap 207).

It mutes the radio's audio here while listening and does not work while transmitting. Needs Android 13 or later; the first time Android may download its English speech model. National alphabets may follow.
- **Edit or delete a logged contact** (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF): a tap on one in the Log tab's list opens it: the call, date and time, frequency, mode, reports, name, comment and the parks, then *Save*, or *Delete* and *Delete it*. It changes this device's log; a copy that already went to Wavelog / Cloudlog, the QRZ.com logbook, the shared ADIF file or Logger32 is not changed there (the dialog names them).
- **× in the call field** clears it, and a **Name** field beside Comment takes the looked-up first name until you type one; it is logged as ADIF NAME (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF).

- **POTA self-spot from the Log tab** (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF): with **My park** set to POTA and your park's reference typed, **Self-spot** beside it shows what goes out (*OH6OKSA at US-1234, 14285.0 kHz SSB*, the transmit slice's frequency and mode, FT8 or FT4 while FTx runs), takes a comment if you like, and on **Spot** posts it to pota.app as a public spot under your call; POTA's answer shows under the row. - **The KPA1500's Tune in STANDBY** (thanks Rich Yost N2RY): Rich's log showed the tune carrier going out at about 775 W for the last 0.7 s of each tune: once the ATU had tuned, the amplifier was back in OPERATE and amplified the carrier the app still held to read the SWR. Tune now puts the amplifier in STANDBY first, as the RF2K-S's Tune does, stops the carrier if it ever reads OPERATE, and after the tune says *Put it in OPERATE when you are ready*. Also from upstream: a late spectrum or waterfall packet no longer drops the frame being drawn, and a small packet to the radio every 5 s keeps a firewall's return path for the spectrum and audio open.
- **RADE and the voice keyer** (thanks Christoph Steinert DM5HF): F1–F12 played the radio's own voice keyer (SmartSDR+'s DVK) while RADE was on, and the radio sends that as analog audio, so it went out as plain SSB on a RADE frequency.
